## 1. Entry rules and scope

Age is a routing input, not an ability score or known school grade. The non-overlapping boundaries—6–7, 8–10, 11–12, 13–14 and 15–16—are project choices. Curriculum references are stored separately. Missing or out-of-range ages must not be silently routed. Candidates and their order are fixed within a band; refresh does not regenerate them. Language changes wording, not materials or requirements.

These are 25 replacement first-visit tasks, not a reconstruction of every legacy M1–M9 item or a complete art curriculum. The five families are observation, expression, figural fragments, object redesign and visual communication. No task requires multiple canvases, movable starter objects or timed phases. Diverse alternatives and improvement of an existing design remain separate extensions; fragments and redesign are not presented as equivalent substitutes. All tasks are research adaptations, not original textbook or PISA test items. [ECD04; PISA23; URBAN05]

“Frozen” means that content and material versions are fixed; it does not claim completed teacher review, child pilots or psychometric validation. No such records are invented. Curriculum and assessment sources inform design; age routing and complexity choices remain project specifications requiring empirical examination.

## 3. Shared delivery and post-task settings

- Learners see the title, prompt, requirements and any hint they request. Research metadata is not extra reading for children. Analysis must not add requirements absent from the learner-facing text.
- Every task starts on a blank canvas and produces one image. References remain in a separate visible area and are not pasted into submitted work. Fragment tasks use manual reproduction from that area and do not require image import or locked layers.
- A 4:3 export is suggested. All reference PNGs are 1600×1200 and must use contain, never cover-cropping. Preserve stems, wheels and object boundaries. Record any different canvas conditions rather than claiming equivalence.
- There is no 60-second phase, countdown, hard time limit or speed score. Existing elapsed-time, pause and help logs may be saved; absent logs must not be imputed as zero.
- Hints are fixed, on-request process support and cannot add requirements. Save which hint was shown and when. Hint use is neither proof of inability nor an unassisted condition.
- All tasks allow monochrome work and do not require writing. The specified image, relationship or purpose is the content requirement; colour richness and word count are not universal completion thresholds.
- Persist the first image, task snapshot and asset version before showing completion. Interest, perceived difficulty and task reflection are skippable. Saving and ending must not wait for model feedback.
- Continue using your existing artwork scorer. Supply the actual image and relevant task context. Dimension associations are not scores or weights and do not automatically modify the rubric. Keep global artwork evaluation separate from task-content analysis.
- Feedback should cite visible evidence, encourage, and offer at most one suggestion without introducing hidden requirements. Do not invent intentions or infer stable weaknesses from one drawing or one choice.
- Additional revision is disabled by default for the first visit. If the existing product enables one optional revision, preserve the first image, feedback and revised image separately and mark assistance. Never overwrite the initial response.

### B1_OB_01 · Two Leaves

**Age and family**: Ages 6–7 / Observation and representation

**Card summary**: Notice how the two leaves differ.

**Learner prompt (always visible)**: Look at the two leaves in the reference and draw both. Show which one is broad and which one is long, and add the stems and veins you can see. A line drawing is fine.

**Requirements (matching the prompt)**: Draw both leaves from the reference. Show their different outlines and include visible stems and veins.

**On-request hints**: H1: Look at the outside edges first, then the lines inside. / H2: You may draw the leaves fairly large.

**Creative freedom**: Use lines or colour. Choose the scale and arrangement; individual veins need not be exact.

**Materials and use**: [Independent reference PNG](../assets/B1_OB_ref.png). Reference: keep available throughout the task and display without cropping.

**Learning intention**: Compare the outlines and local features of natural objects.

**Complexity descriptors (not calibrated difficulty)**: Two separate objects; no occlusion requirement; observation from an image rather than life.

**Nine-dimensional associations (task metadata, not scores)**

- Focus · Realism (`realism`): A shared reference allows comparison of outline and visible features, not a complete measure of drawing from life.
- Focus · Line Combination (`line_combination`): The task explicitly involves outer edges and internal vein lines.
- Exploratory · Picture Organization (`picture_organization`): The arrangement of the leaves is observable, but positions are not prescribed.
- Exploratory · Color Richness (`color_richness`): Relevant only if the learner chooses to use colour.

**Task-content analysis**: Look for differentiated outlines, stem connections and vein placement. Record the chosen representation rather than expecting imitation of the illustration style.

**Interpretation limits**: A monochrome response is not a task failure. Copying an illustration does not establish botanical knowledge.

**Optional post-task reflection**: Where do you think the two leaves differ most?

**Submission and post-task configuration**: Save one initial image; use first_visit_common_v1. Interest and perceived difficulty are each skippable; reflection is optional. Ending does not depend on feedback completion.

**Source location**: [C22], [YIN22] · Grades 1–2: personal expression, improving everyday objects and visual play

**Explicit adaptation**: The curriculum informs natural observation. The two-leaf contrast and this illustration are project choices, not textbook stimuli.
